Where I come from

It started in my family's shop.

I was 13, working in my family's Apple consulting business, and I liked taking computers apart to understand them. I taught myself the rest.

Thirty-odd years later I've sat in CTO and CIO seats and co-founded a few companies. Then I walked away from a startup I'd co-founded, because the automations we were selling were built to replace people. I wanted to use AI for people instead.

That's still the whole plan.

The part I care about most

Teaching, mostly around a table.

I teach AI for free to the people around me, and I run workshops and cohorts for developers, entrepreneurs and directors, at nonprofits and businesses alike.

One person or a full room, it goes the same way: we start from your own work, we try things, and you leave able to do it without me. I'd rather teach your people than do it for them and leave.

nobody gets left behind at step two.

How I can help

Tell me what you're trying to do.

Put AI to work in your company

Assistants and agents wired into your real systems, data and daily routines, using whatever tools your team already runs. Not a demo.

Teach your team

Developers or not. We talk with a few of your people first, set up accounts and privacy properly, then learn together on your own work.

Build the software around it

The ordinary kind, built to last: back ends, APIs, data pipelines, web and desktop apps, from the first idea to production.

Be your part-time CTO

Someone to help with technical decisions, hiring and a roadmap your team can really ship, without a full-time executive salary.

It starts with a 30-minute call. If one of my products already solves your problem, I'll point you there instead.

Good work deserves good tools.

I work at lower rates for nonprofits with a positive impact on the world, like the Arctic Eider Society, where I help bring more AI into SIKU, its Indigenous knowledge platform.

What I've made

A few things I built because someone needed them.

Wspr

Private voice to text on your desktop, powered by local AI. Your words stay on your machine.

MidMeeting

Your own AI in your meetings, with no bot on the call. A quiet note when something is worth catching.

Lantern

Human-reviewed instructions for AI assistants, for nonprofits and small teams. Made with Nat Hollands at Both Hands.

CortexMind

Long-term memory for AI assistants, so they remember your work from one day to the next.

More on GitHub, including the free tools I keep for anyone who wants them.
